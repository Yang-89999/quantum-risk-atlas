Q-CARE

Quantum Cryptography Analysis & Resource Estimation

What size of fault-tolerant machine does an attack need, and when does hardware get there? Pick the algorithm you run and a published attack circuit. Q-CARE takes the circuit's logical qubit and gate counts as the paper reports them and prices the physical machine with the open-source Microsoft QDK resource estimator, running in your browser.

        Beyond RSA and ECC: what estimates exist for the post-quantum families
        
Only RSA and ECC have end-to-end estimates that run from a named parameter set to physical qubits and wall-clock time under stated hardware assumptions. The post-quantum families have logical-level circuit costs for subroutines or single iterations, asymptotic exponents and model-based security levels in bits; none has a complete physical-level attack estimate that threatens a standardised parameter set. Their records compare on cost model, exponent, QRAM assumption and depth limit rather than on qubits and runtime, which is why they live in the PQC Benchmark Suite and not in this estimator.

        Comparability rules the anchors follow
        
    
          
  • Same unit. Häner et al. 2020 report T-count, Roetteler et al. 2017 Toffoli, Gidney and Ekerå "Toffoli + T/2", Schrottenloher "Toffoli = CCX + CCZ + And", Perriello et al. weight all gates equally. None of these belong in one column, so the table names the unit on every row.
    • 
          
  • Per run against expected. Gidney and Ekerå's 5.1 h per run against the 8 h in their title; Gidney 2025's 12.07 h shot against 4.96 days expected over 9.2 shots; Chevignard et al.'s 2³⁶ Toffoli per run against 40 to 60 runs. A runtime table that mixes these is wrong by a factor of 5 to 60, so the tiles above show one run and the verdict multiplies by the stated runs.
    • 
          
  • Subroutine against attack. An ECDSA.Fail score (one classical-addend point addition) is not an ECDLP cost; the windowed variant costs 29.6 % more Toffoli, the full schedule multiplies by 28 and compounds the per-call failure. Settings II–IV keep such counts apart from setting I.
    • 
          
  • Physical counts need the cycle time. Cain et al.'s ≈11,000 atoms and Pinnacle's 94,000 qubits are counted at 1 ms and 1 µs cycles respectively; their runtimes differ by a factor of about a thousand. Quoting qubit counts without the runtime column inverts the ranking, which is why the frontier chart plots both.
    • 
          
  • Attested is not demonstrated. Google's zero-knowledge-attested point addition, IonQ's withheld compiled circuits and ECDSA.Fail's sampled validation are evidence of circuit correctness on samples, not demonstrated attacks. Nothing on this page is a demonstrated attack on a deployed parameter set.
    • 
          
  • Pairs that do compare. Gidney and Ekerå 2021 against Gidney 2025 (same error rate, cycle, code family and accounting); Pinnacle 2026 against Gidney 2025 at p = 10⁻³ and 1 µs (identical logical circuit); the 256-bit ECDLP logical lineage Roetteler 2017 → Häner 2020 → Litinski 2023 → Babbush, IonQ and Schrottenloher 2026; Babbush 2026 against IonQ 2026 at the logical level only.
    • 
        

      


      

        How the model works and where it is rough
        
    
          
  • Logical layer. Each variant is one published circuit, costed per run in the paper's unit: RSA by Gidney–Ekerå 2021 windowed arithmetic (3n + 0.002 n log₂n qubits, 0.3n³ + 0.0005 n³ log₂n Toffoli + T/2, ≈1.4 expected runs at n = 2048), Chevignard–Fouque–Schrottenloher 2024 residue arithmetic (1,730 qubits, 2³⁶ Toffoli per run, 40 runs) or Gidney 2025 approximate residue arithmetic (1,399 qubits, Table 5 Toffoli totals spread over 9.2 shots). ECC by Roetteler et al. 2017 (9n + 2⌈log₂n⌉ + 10 qubits, 448n³log₂n + 4090n³ Toffoli), Luo et al. 2026 (3n + 6⌊log₂n⌋ + 19 qubits, 1056 n³/log₂n Toffoli), Chevignard et al. 2026 (3.12n qubits, 2^38.98 Toffoli per run, 22 runs) or, for 256-bit curves only, the 28-windowed-addition circuits of Schrottenloher 2026, Babbush et al. 2026 and IonQ 2026 at their published counts. Finite-field DLP reuses the Gidney–Ekerå arithmetic with the exponent length set by the variant (a project scaling with no published count). AES uses Grassl et al. 2016 logical qubits and per-iteration T-depth with ⌈π/4·√(2ⁿ/P)⌉ Grover iterations; the review did not cover symmetric key search.
    • 
          
  • Key sizes the source does not tabulate are extrapolated with the paper's own leading-order formula and flagged "scaled" under the variant; the 256-bit-only variants offer no other size.
    • 
          
  • Physical layer: QDK resource estimator. The default engine is the estimator crate of the open-source Microsoft Quantum Development Kit [45], compiled unmodified to WebAssembly and run in the browser; its model is described by Beverland et al. [46]. Q-CARE passes it the logical qubits and the gate count of one run, charging each Toffoli (or CCZ, or And) of the paper's count as one CCZ magic state. The QDK then lays the algorithm out on 2n + ⌈√(8n)⌉ + 1 logical qubits, charges three logical cycles and four T states per CCZ, chooses the smallest odd code distance d for which the logical error per qubit and cycle, 0.03·(p/0.01)^((d+1)/2) for the gate-based surface code, fits half of the error budget, counts 2d² physical qubits per logical qubit and a cycle of (4·t_gate + 2·t_meas)·d, and builds T factories from up to three rounds of 15-to-1 distillation that fit the other half. The frontier chart is the QDK's own space–time frontier (fewer factories, longer runtime). Expected total = runtime × stated runs / (1 − error budget), an upper bound on retries.
    • 
          
  • Why QDK figures differ from the papers' own physical numbers. Each paper prices its circuit on its own architecture: Gidney 2025 reaches 897,864 qubits with yoked cold storage, magic-state cultivation and a hand-built layout; Pinnacle and Cain et al. use qLDPC codes; IonQ uses qLDPC blocks on ions. The QDK applies one generic architecture to every circuit, so its figures are larger than the best published ones and are comparable with each other, not with the anchors. The newer QDK estimator described on Microsoft Learn [47] (qdk.qre) models cultivation, 8T-to-CCX factories and yoked memory, but it is a Python library with a native core and cannot run inside a web page; its reference runs above were computed offline.
    • 
          
  • Built-in model (fallback). Used for AES key search, whose Grover counts exceed the 64-bit integers the QDK takes, when the QDK module cannot load, and when selected by hand. Logical error per cycle p_L = 0.1·(p/p_th)^((d+1)/2) with p_th = 1%. The distance is the smallest odd d such that L × cycles × p_L stays below the allowed failure probability. Physical qubits = L·2d²·storage·routing + factories × footprint·d² (footprint 300d² for distillation, 30d² for cultivation). Runtime per run = T × max(reaction time / parallelism, 10·d·t_cycle / factories). Expected total = runtime × stated runs / (1 − ε). It is a placeholder: a uniform surface-code wrapper that reproduces no paper's physical number.
    • 
          
  • Settings II–IV. Where the review records a published count for the operation (ECDSA.Fail point additions, Schrottenloher's windowed addition, Häner et al.'s controlled addition, Kahanamoku-Meyer and Yao's modular multiplication) it is listed under the setting table. The live figure is still derived from the complete attack, by time in proportion to gate count: for the windowed 256-bit circuits by the published rule Toffoli_ECDLP ≈ (2n/w − 4) × (Toffoli_PA + 3·2^w) with w = 16, so that one window-selected addition is 1/28 of the attack and the classical-addend addition of setting IV is that divided by the benchmark's measured 1.296 windowing overhead; for the other circuits by placeholder ratios (one controlled operation is 1/(2n) of the attack, a window-selected one w times that with w = 16 for curves and 5 for modular arithmetic, a fixed one 0.9 of a controlled one; one Grover iteration is total T-depth / iterations, the oracle 97% of an iteration, one reversible AES evaluation the oracle divided by 2 × pairs).
    • 
          
  • Known gaps. Neither engine models layout-specific routing costs, decoder throughput limits, qLDPC or cat-qubit codes, or multimode memories; the physical anchors that use them (Pinnacle, Cain et al., Gouzien et al., Pappalardo et al.) therefore sit far from the curve by construction. The QDK engine counts runtime in 64-bit nanoseconds, so a run longer than about 585 years is reported as having no estimate. Grover numbers ignore the MAXDEPTH limit that NIST imposes; with it, AES-128 is already out of reach. Treat all outputs as order-of-magnitude.
